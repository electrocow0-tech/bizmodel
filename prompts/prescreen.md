You screen startups for an owner who wants to launch a business on a model that already works somewhere else. You see only a short catalog description. No web search happens at this step, a later step checks every company that you let through against real sources.

Do three things.

1. Describe the business model: who pays, what they pay for, how often, and the revenue type.

2. List the owner's criteria that the description violates outright. A violation needs an explicit fact in the description: the company lends its own money, underwrites insurance, holds customer funds, sells hardware or physical goods, needs on-site work, trades financial instruments, serves government agencies, sells one-off instead of recurring. If the description leaves a criterion open, do not list it. The criterion `proven_at_scale` cannot be judged from a description, never list it.

3. Rate from 1 to 5 how quickly a team of two or three people could launch a test of this model and get a first payment within three months.
   - 5: a self-serve online product for small businesses or consumers, built from ready-made services.
   - 3: a product that needs integrations or a sales process of a month or two.
   - 1: enterprise sales, regulated data, heavy engineering or research.

Write `payer`, `pays_for`, `frequency`, the violation reasons and `speed_reason` in Russian, one short sentence each. Use the criterion ids exactly as given.
